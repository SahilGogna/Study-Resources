# The Referral Playbook

How to get referrals from people who do not know you.

## Start here

A referral is not a favour you extract. It is a small professional risk someone takes on your behalf. Their name goes next to yours inside their company.

Everything in this playbook exists to make that risk feel small.

Twenty people you actually researched will beat a thousand you did not. If you only take one thing from this page, take that.

## Part 1. Who to actually message

### The mistake

Most people message directors, VPs and heads of department. Those people get dozens of these every week, have never been in your position, and have the most to lose by referring a stranger.

### Who actually refers

The person most likely to refer you is one to three years into the role you want, at the company you want, and got there on a path that looks like yours.

Four signals to look for:

1. **One to three years in the role.** New enough to remember the struggle. Established enough to have referral privileges.
2. **Shares a path with you.** Same college, same program, same country of origin, or the same career switch you are making.
3. **Joined recently.** People who joined in the last year are often still inside a referral bonus window, and they are actively looking for candidates.
4. **Posts about their team hiring.** If they posted "my team is hiring" in the last month, they want to hear from you. Message them today.

### How to find them on LinkedIn

**Method 1. The alumni filter.** This is the best one and almost nobody uses it.

- Go to your college's LinkedIn page
- Click **Alumni**
- Under "Where they work", type the target company
- Under "What they studied", pick your program

You now have a list of people who did your degree and work where you want to work. That is the warmest cold list you will ever build.

**Method 2. People search filters.**

- LinkedIn search, **People** tab
- Current company: your target company
- School: your school
- Locations: the city you are targeting
- Connections: 2nd

**Method 3. Keyword search for active referrers.**

Search these phrases and filter to the last month under Posts:

- `my team is hiring`
- `we are hiring` plus the role title
- `referral` plus the company name

People who post this are telling you they want inbound. Go to them first.

### Who not to message

- Directors, VPs, heads of anything. They do not refer strangers.
- Recruiters, for a referral. That is a different channel for a different ask.
- Anyone with fifteen years at the company who never walked a path like yours.

### How many

Build a list of 20. Five new people a day, five days a week. Track them in a sheet with four columns: name, company, date messaged, reply.

## Part 2. The messages

### The five rules

1. One message, complete. Never send "hi" and wait.
2. Say who you are, what you want, and why them. Four lines.
3. Never attach your resume in the first message.
4. Never ask for a referral in the first message.
5. One follow up. There is no second one.

### Template 1. Connection request note

Under 300 characters, which is the LinkedIn limit.

```
Hi [Name], I am a [program] grad in [city] targeting [role] roles. I saw you
moved from [their background] into [their role] at [company], which is close
to the move I am trying to make. Would be great to connect and learn from how
you did it.
```

### Template 2. First message after they accept

```
Hi [Name], thanks for connecting.

Quick context. I finished [program] at [college] and I am targeting [role]
roles in [city]. Right now I am working on [one specific project or skill].

I noticed you made a similar move into [company] and I would really like to
hear how you approached it.

No ask attached. I am just trying to learn from people a few steps ahead of me.
```

### Template 3. The coffee chat ask

Send this after one or two exchanges, never cold.

```
Would you be open to a 15 minute call in the next couple of weeks?

I would mainly want to ask about [specific thing one] and [specific thing two].
Happy to work around whatever time suits you, and I will keep it to 15 minutes.
```

### Template 4. The one follow up

One week later. Once. Then you stop.

```
Hi [Name], floating this back up in case it got buried. Completely understand
if now is not a good time. Either way, thanks for connecting.
```

### Template 5. Asking for a referral without a chat

Use this when a role opens at their company and you have no relationship yet. It is a long shot, but the honesty is what makes it work.

```
Hi [Name], [company] just posted a [role] opening, req [number].

I am a [program] grad with [one line of relevant proof]. Resume attached in
case it is useful.

Would you be open to referring me? Completely understand if you would rather
not, given we have not worked together. Either way I appreciate you taking
a look.
```

### Template 6. Cold email to a hiring manager

```
Subject: [Role] opening, req [number]

Hi [Name],

I saw your team is hiring a [role]. I am a [program] grad in [city] with
[one specific, relevant thing].

One thing I built that is relevant: [one line, with a link].

I have applied through the portal. If it helps, I am happy to send more
detail or answer anything directly.

Thanks for your time,
[Your name]
```

### Template 7. Cold email to a recruiter

```
Subject: Application for [role], req [number]

Hi [Name],

I applied for the [role] posting, req [number]. Quick summary in case it
helps you place me:

[Role] with [X] years in [domain]
[The one credential or project that matters most]
Based in [city], [work authorization status]

Happy to answer anything. Thanks for taking a look.

[Your name]
```

### A note on cold email

Email is the weakest of the three channels for referrals from strangers. You usually cannot find the address, and a stranger's inbox is a colder room than their LinkedIn.

It earns its place for hiring managers and recruiters on a specific open role. That is a different play from a referral. Use LinkedIn for referrals and email for live reqs.

### What gets you ignored

- "Hi" with nothing after it
- A wall of text with no line breaks
- "I am looking for any opportunity." Any means you have not thought about it
- Resume attached before any conversation has happened
- Asking for a referral in message one

## Part 3. The coffee chat

### The rule that matters most

Show up.

If you asked for the time and you do not appear, that person is gone permanently. In a market like Toronto your name travels. One no show costs you more than a hundred ignored messages ever will.

### Before the call

- Accept the invite the moment it lands
- Put it in your calendar with a 30 minute reminder
- Read their profile properly. Know their path
- Write your three questions down before you join

### The 15 minute structure

| Time | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| 0 to 2 min | Thank them. 30 second version of your background |
| 2 to 10 min | Your three questions |
| 10 to 13 min | Ask about their team and what they screen for |
| 13 to 15 min | The close |

### Questions that work

- What did your first six months in this role actually look like?
- What do people underestimate about breaking into this?
- If you were me right now, what would you be spending your time on?
- What does your team actually screen for on a resume?

### Questions that kill it

- "Can you refer me?" in the first two minutes
- "What is the salary?"
- "Can you review my resume?" Too big an ask for someone who just met you

### How to close

```
This was really helpful, thank you. If a role on your team ever opens up that
fits, would you be open to me reaching out?
```

Read that again. You are not asking for a referral. You are asking for permission to ask later.

Almost nobody says no to that, and now you have a door that stays open.

### After the call

**Same day thank you.**

```
Thanks for the 15 minutes today [Name], genuinely useful. The point about
[specific thing] was the one I needed to hear. I am going to work on [the
thing] over the next couple of weeks.
```

**The two week update.** This is the single most underused move in this entire playbook.

```
Hi [Name], quick update. You mentioned [thing], so I [what you did].
[One line on the result.] Thanks again for pointing me at it.
```

Most people vanish after the chat. The ones who come back with proof they acted on the advice are the ones who get referred. That message is what turns a stranger into somebody willing to put their name next to yours.

## Your first 30 days

| Week | What you do |
| --- | --- |
| Week 1 | Build the list of 20 using the alumni filter |
| Week 2 | Connection notes, then first messages |
| Week 3 | Coffee chats. Show up to every single one |
| Week 4 | Thank yous, two week updates, first referral asks |

## Last thing

All of this fails the moment you treat it as volume.

The people getting referrals are not sending more messages than you. They are sending twenty better ones to twenty better chosen people, and then they are showing up.
